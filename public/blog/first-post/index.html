Stream of Conciousness

June 25, 2025

Motivation

It’s officially time that I start this thing that I have been thinking about for so long now, a blog. In the past few years, it’s become obvious to me and many other people that building a following is going to become increasingly valuable as time goes on. Because of this, rather than starting a shitty TikTok channel or a brainless and faceless Youtube channel like others suggested, I figured that to start, a blog would make the most sense. I hope that this blog will give me an outlet to improve upon my writing, while connecting with blog readers (a community I believe to be much more interesting than tik tok watchers). With this blog idea lingering for the past year or so, you may ask yourself why hasn’t he started earlier, and the truth to that question lies in the fact that I am lazy and fearful of putting skin in the game.

Anyways back to the value of building a following: I think that I could give a list of ten justifying reasons to support this idea that followings will become much more valuable in the future, however in hopes of keeping things short I’ll only mention a few. Firstly and most simply, I think that we have already seen this happen over the course of my lifetime and most obviously to me in the last 10 or so years. Things like streamers and influencers had some early adopters before this 10 year window, however the sheer increase from then to now is something that everyone has felt. Along with this, the amount of money that these creators are generating in total has gone up by quite a lot.

In layman’s terms, more people are consuming content, thus more people are creating content, and thus more value is being generated by these content creators.

Therefore, it follows from this trend over the past decade or so that a similar trend will continue and creators will only become more prevalent.

Another reason that followings will be more and more valuable as time goes on is because of technology and the rate at which it is improving. As will become clear in the posts I make on here later, I am an AI doomer of sorts, and more generally a doomer as a whole, but AI and other technology is affecting society quite a bit. Because of this effect now and the hypothesized effect soon to come, people have proposed things like UBI that aim to level the playing field for humans and their quality of life as they begin to be substituted out of “the loop”. If such a thing as UBI were to happen, or if humans come out of the loop in some large way, the only real avenue of meaning that will be left is to please other humans, with the most sensical way to do that being content creation. In this case, the demand for content creation rises (rather marginally I believe) however the supply of content creators rises more drastically therefore making it much more valuable to have a following as the size of your following relative to others has gotten bigger. Now I know I know, this dystopian fantasy seems specific and super far fetched, but I also could see many adjacent cases in which a similar thing happens, and the value of followings also increases.

Lastly, I think it has to be said that as creators become more and more plentiful, the amount of garbage “brainrot” content becomes more plentiful as well. Because of this, I think that building meaningful communities of non brainrot interaction comes at such a premium, even if the monetary or numerical compensation isn’t as great.

Anyways, enough for my spiel about followings and why I want to blog, let’s get into some more interesting stuff, like a review and interpretation of some interesting things that I saw today and some housekeeping stuff about my personal life.

Cluely PWNd

He said that “it’s just so easy to go viral these days” because the supply just doesn’t match the demand ever since people started going crazy for short form content. While I definitely agree with him a little bit, I also think that it’s clear that he has a knack for this whole virality thing. Maybe its god given talent, maybe its hard work, but whatever it is, he knows what the fuck he’s doing.

Secondly, he seems to be going more viral than ever, mostly capturing the momentum from this insane seed round. His announcement video had maybe the best balance of actual substance, and shock value/ragebait. Ive seen so many comparison clips of Roy’s interview style and young Zucks interview style, but if I had to make a prediction, I would say that in the end his business will fizzle out although I think he will be able to hold the spotlight for a long time, and potentially think of a more interesting and long term business (no offense to Cluely).

Another interesting development in the Roy Lee story arc, is the large group of people I see on twitter, trying to gotcha him. Both the distributors of these models and the companies using them all face the same issue. Here Roy is explaining some small design choice about how he stored and retrieved his system prompt (I believe he hard coded it into the client or something like that).

That being said, this tweet just blows my mind because it shows just how sophisticated Roy Lee’s self proclaimed “rizz” is, but also just how gigabrain his marketing strategies are. In all seriousness this could’ve been a totally unplanned development internally at Cluely, but the fact that they see it as a positive way of “marketing” their product really makes me think that this shock value/brainrot marketing is gonna become the new meta.

Tesla and Robotaxi vs Waymo & Geohot

Geohot conversation

Next on my topic list is a little discussion of the tesla robotaxi rollout in Austin this past week. From what I’ve heard on twitter it seems like they killed it. Don’t get me wrong, Twitter is probably the most biased source of Elon related news, but seeing videos and live streams from the event, my mind sort of got blown. I think through it all, the only limiting factor about this launch that prevents me from yoloing my networth into tesla calls is the fact that there was a person sitting in the front seat (although it was unclear what their real purpose was) and the fact that the area of service was so small even compared to waymo. The person in the front seat thing shouldn’t be a long term issue for the scalability of the robotaxis, as long as there is no foul play with human in the loop developments, like what went down at the humanoid robot event in Silicon Valley earlier this year (they were totally human controlled). Along with this, the small area of service begs a similar question for me, where I am unsure how manicured the actual software was to handle that small sample size of roads rather than using a more general computer vision model. Only time (and leaks) will tell with these issues but don’t get me wrong if these aren’t too bad, I think that Uber and Waymo are in for a tough next few years battling Tesla in the scale wars. I Also think that if those potential issues are not catastrophic that this event will be the beginning of the answer in terms of the Lidar vs Computer Vision debate.

This analysis of the Tesla robotaxi soft launch leads me into my next big topic which is Geohot (George Hotz) and his segment on the TBPN podcast network this past week. Geohot has long been my favorite person in the tech world because of his give no fucks attitude and contrarian opinions that I often find to be more logical predictions than that of the crowds. This week, he said a few things that really itched my brain. The first, and most applicable to the Tesla vs. Waymo’s argument was that Waymo actually has ~1.2 humans per car remotely controlling the actions of this car. This totally changed my robotaxi/driverless car worldview because for so long I was under the impression that driverless cars almost “just worked”, however it’s now clear to me that we’re a bit farther from the solution than I thought we were. This also leads me to believe with even more conviction that Elon has always known what we are doing with the robotaxis and the whole game is really his for the taking. I think this because firstly, the tesla robotaxis we saw this past week cost ~40k so it seems, and from what people in the cars saw, they had no alterations or cameras attached to them, that is, they were simply using FSD. However, these Waymos that have ~1.2 people in the loop per car (MORE THAN AN UBER) cost closer to 250k and do the same thing. Because of this, when we talk about scalability, even if Tesla is also remotely controlling their vehicles with a human in the loop or even if there is a major accident in the news, I still think that no one else has a chance.

It’s important to note here that while I haven’t done my due diligence on any of these numbers that George Hotz cited, I definitely put a lot of trust in him because of his track record. For example, in this appearance he had on TBPN, they asked him “How are things at comma going” and to that he responded quite happily, “We are ~2 years behind Tesla”. I mean not only the honesty is impressive but the awareness to say such a thing so frankly just blows my mind. On a similar note, he also expressed his distaste in the whole “protect your company so that you can win” idea by encouraging anyone to challenge him. I mean he just knows that what he is doing is special and that really no one can do it but him. All of this together just makes him such a role model for me because he has such confidence in himself and yet with that confidence he also brings such honesty and novelty to this world of egotistical fucks looking to raise the next $1b rather than creating something actually good.

The next big thing that Geohot mentioned that was one of my biggest takeaways from this week of content consumption was the AI hype hierarchy. He first dove into the bullshittery that comes with words like AGI and agents which are constantly regarded as the future, with people saying “AGI NEXT YEAR” or “2025, the year of agents”. “What’s an agent” George repeated, “what’s AGI”. These questions that really no one has a good answer to should be the starting point of our understanding of AI, not the end. After this subtle rant about the ambiguity of these words, he got into the landscape of AI startups and companies. He broke it down into I believe 4 tiers. Tier 4 are the AI wrappers as people call them. These companies may be doing some subtle fine tuning or may be creative in the way that they prompt these models, but in reality, their success is 100% dependent on the functionality of the models, and the decisions made by the companies who make them (excluding open source for now). This specific realization was my favorite of the entire week. Now, when I think about companies like that of our favorite viral machine Roy Lee, I now realize that if OpenAI or Anthropic or Google or really any other significant private or open source player in the LLM game wanted to make something with similar functionality to Cluely (maybe because they realize that people desire such a thing), it makes his company obsolete. This moat issue can be generalized to so many more companies and really changes the way that I think about valuing companies focused on AI. The next tier George mentioned was tier 3, in this tier, he noted companies like OpenAI and Anthropic, the companies who provide these models. Interestingly, he reasoned that in the future, even these companies may lack a moat in his opinion. Next was tier 2 and 1, with tier 2 including companies like nvidia that make the hardware, and tier 1 being more low level things like datacenters and fabs (if I understood correctly). Anyways, without getting into the weeds about all of these tiers and what I think about them, I think that specifically, this thought experiment regarding the tier 4 companies reframed a lot of my prior beliefs that lead me to believe that certain companies were very valuable.
